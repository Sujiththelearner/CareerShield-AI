How to Spot Fake Internships & Job Scams

Learn the top 6 deceptive techniques cybercriminals use to target university students seeking internships and early-career jobs.

Threat Level: CRITICAL
1. The "Equipment Deposit" Trap

The recruiter sends an official-looking offer letter claiming to provide an Apple MacBook, monitor, and office supplies. They demand an advance "refundable security deposit" (usually ₹2,000 to ₹5,000) for shipping or courier insurance via Google Pay / UPI.

Golden Rule: Legitimate enterprises (Google, Infosys, Microsoft, TCS) NEVER ask candidates to pay for company hardware or courier logistics. Any request for money is 100% fraud.
Threat Level: HIGH
2. Domain Impersonation (Spoofing)

A recruiter claims to be from a Fortune 500 company or prominent tech brand, but contacts you from a generic webmail address like amazon.recruitment.hr@gmail.com or a newly created knockoff domain (e.g. careers-microsoft-jobs.net).

Golden Rule: Real corporate HR teams only write from verifiable enterprise domains (e.g. @amazon.com). Check domain WHOIS registration dates and corporate career directories.
Threat Level: HIGH
3. Telegram / WhatsApp Only Interviews

You apply on LinkedIn or Indeed, and within hours receive a message telling you to message a hiring manager on Telegram or WhatsApp. The entire "interview" is conducted via text messages, and you are offered a job within 30 minutes without face-to-face video or technical assessment.

Golden Rule: Professional tech firms schedule calendar invites on Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, or Zoom. Fraudsters use Telegram for anonymity and untraceable accounts.
Threat Level: CRITICAL
4. The "Daily Task Payout" Scam

Advertises "Part-Time Media Associate" or "YouTube Video Rating Assistant" paying ₹2,000–₹5,000 per day for simple clicks. They pay you ₹150 for the first task to build trust, then demand a deposit into a "crypto wallet / task pool" to unlock larger payouts.

Golden Rule: Low-skill tasks paying exorbitant daily sums are money-laundering schemes. Never deposit personal funds to receive "earned wages".
Threat Level: MEDIUM
5. Paid Training / Mandatory Certification

The company offers an internship but states that before joining, you must complete their paid training module or purchase a specific certification exam for ₹3,000 to ₹10,000.

Golden Rule: Genuine internships provide on-the-job training paid by the employer, never paid by the intern.
Threat Level: HIGH
6. Immediate Identity Harvesting

Before an initial screening, the recruiter asks for your Aadhaar card number, PAN card, bank account details, and passport scans under the guise of "HR background check".

Golden Rule: Identity documents are only submitted after an official offer letter has been verified and signed, typically on your official start date through a company HR portal.
